# SENSEMAP v0.1 — Product and Technical Design

**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Status:** Approved design, pending implementation plan  
**Working tagline:** *Know the place before you go.*

## 1. Purpose

SENSEMAP is a free, public, map-based service that helps people understand what a place may feel like before they go there. It is designed especially with autistic people and people with sensory or accessibility needs in mind, while remaining useful to anyone who benefits from information about noise, lighting, crowding, seating, toilets, accessibility, quiet areas, strong smells, queues, and similar environmental characteristics.

SENSEMAP must describe environments rather than label places as objectively “safe” or “unsafe”. The product should help users decide whether a place fits their own needs.

## 2. Product principles

1. **Free for everyone.** No premium tier, paywall, or advertising.
2. **Useful without an account.** Searching, browsing, filtering, and viewing place information require no login.
3. **Account only for contribution.** Login is required to submit or edit community data and write text comments.
4. **Privacy first.** Sensory preferences should be stored locally by default. Precise user location should not be retained merely to provide nearby results.
5. **Descriptive, not judgmental.** Present concrete attributes and confidence instead of a universal safety score.
6. **Community-powered, evidence-aware.** Multiple independent reports should be combined rather than allowing one user to define a place.
7. **Open foundations.** Prefer open-source components and avoid unnecessary vendor lock-in.
8. **Nynorsk and English from day one.** Both are first-class product languages.

## 3. Core user experience

### 3.1 Home screen

The default experience is **map first**.

At the top:

- Large search field: “Søk etter stad eller behov…” / “Search for a place or need…”
- Quick-filter chips for:
  - Noise
  - Lighting
  - Crowds
  - Quiet area
  - Accessibility
  - More filters

The rest of the screen is primarily the map.

Bottom navigation:

- **Map / Kart**
- **For me / For meg**
- **Contribute / Bidra**

### 3.2 Map pins

Pins should not use a simple red/green good/bad model.

Instead, map markers communicate data coverage and relevance:

- Place with substantial SENSEMAP data
- Place with limited SENSEMAP data
- OpenStreetMap place with no SENSEMAP data yet
- Optional visual indicator for a particularly strong personal match

### 3.3 Search modes

SENSEMAP supports two complementary search modes:

**Place search**

Examples:
- Førde bibliotek
- Norwich Cathedral

**Need-based search**

Examples:
- stille kafé
- bibliotek med roleg område
- quiet café with dim lighting
- wheelchair accessible café

Need-based queries are parsed into structured filters rather than treated only as free text.

## 4. Personal needs profile

Users may optionally configure “Kva hjelper deg?” / “What helps you?”.

Examples:

- Prefer low noise
- Avoid strong lighting
- Crowds are acceptable
- Need seating
- Prefer a quiet retreat area

These preferences are stored locally by default and do not require an account.

SENSEMAP may calculate a personal fit such as:

> “Very good match for your needs”

A numerical percentage may be used if it is clearly presented as a **personal fit score**, never a safety score.

## 5. Place profile — “Know before you go”

A place profile should communicate its environment at a glance.

Core attributes for v0.1:

- Noise level
- Lighting
- Crowding
- Quiet/retreat area
- Seating
- Toilets
- Accessibility
- Entrance/exit clarity
- Strong smells
- Queues/waiting

Temperature/indoor climate may be collected when relevant, but is secondary to the core list.

Each category can show:

- Typical conditions
- Current/recent conditions where live reports exist
- Number of reports
- Recency
- Confidence / amount of supporting data

Example:

> **Noise**  
> Usually quiet  
> Right now: moderate  
> 4 reports in the last 2 hours

## 6. Three classes of place information

SENSEMAP separates data into three lifetimes.

### 6.1 Persistent facts

Examples:

- Step-free entrance
- Toilet available
- Dedicated quiet room
- Seating available

These persist until changed, disputed, or superseded.

### 6.2 Typical conditions

Examples:

- Usually crowded on Friday afternoons
- Usually quiet before 14:00

These are aggregated from reports over time and may vary by day/time.

### 6.3 Live reports

Examples:

- Quiet right now
- Very crowded right now

These have an explicit expiry time and automatically stop influencing “right now” status after the configured window.

## 7. Contributing data

The contribution flow should be fast enough to complete in under one minute.

Primary action:

**“Korleis er det her?” / “What’s it like here?”**

Initial quick choices:

- Noise: Quiet / Moderate / Loud
- Crowds: Few people / Some people / Busy
- Lighting: Soft / Normal / Strong

Optional structured details:

- Quiet area available
- Toilet available
- Seating available
- Step-free entrance
- Strong smells
- Flashing/changing lights
- Narrow or cramped areas
- Queue/waiting

A short optional comment may be added by logged-in users.

## 8. Source data strategy

SENSEMAP uses a **combined model**:

1. Existing places from OpenStreetMap provide broad map coverage from the start.
2. SENSEMAP stores sensory/accessibility/community information separately.
3. Users can enrich existing places or suggest new/missing places.

OpenStreetMap data and SENSEMAP data must remain logically separate so map refreshes do not overwrite community reports.

## 9. Trust and moderation

### 9.1 Internal trust weighting

Reports receive internal trust weighting based on signals such as:

- Agreement among independent contributors
- Contributor history of reports later confirmed by others
- Report freshness
- Plausible presence near the place for “right now” reports
- Agreement with established time-of-day patterns

Do not expose a gamified public points system.

User-facing confidence wording should remain simple, for example:

- High confidence
- Based on 38 reports
- Limited data — based on 2 reports

### 9.2 Anti-spam controls

v0.1 should include:

- Rate limiting
- Outlier handling so a single report cannot dominate established data
- Login required for text comments
- Report-content function
- Moderator queue for new places and significant changes

Report reasons should include:

- Incorrect information
- Offensive content
- Advertising/spam
- Personal information
- Place no longer exists

### 9.3 Privacy of contributors

Do not publicly show who submitted a specific sensory report.

Display aggregated wording such as:

> “Reported by 6 users.”

Accounts are contribution identities, not social-media profiles.

### 9.4 Trusted contributors

A future or later-v0.x trusted-contributor role may allow reliable users to verify factual attributes. It must not grant unilateral power to censor other people’s legitimate sensory experiences.

## 10. Place owners

A later verified-place feature may allow an organization to provide factual information such as:

- Opening hours
- Entrance information
- Quiet room availability
- Accessibility information
- Sensory-friendly hours
- Contact details

Place owners must never be able to remove or rewrite user experiences merely because they dislike them.

## 11. “This has changed” flow

Every place profile should provide a simple way to report that conditions or facilities have changed.

This is important because renovations, management changes, new lighting, music policies, accessibility improvements, and similar changes can make older reports stale.

## 12. Language and localization

First-class languages:

- **Nynorsk (`nn`)**
- **English (`en`)**

Language selection should be available from first launch, with a device-language suggestion where appropriate.

No user-facing string should be hard-coded in the UI. Use translation files from the beginning, for example:

```text
/locales
  /nn
    common.json
  /en
    common.json
```

The data model stores semantic values independently of language. For example, `noise=quiet` is rendered as “Stille” or “Quiet” depending on locale.

This permits later addition of other languages without schema changes.

## 13. Technical architecture

### 13.1 Web client

Recommended stack:

- React
- TypeScript
- Vite
- Progressive Web App (PWA)
- MapLibre GL JS for interactive maps

The web application is the first shipping client.

### 13.2 Android strategy

After the web/PWA experience is stable, package the shared application using Capacitor for Android.

The goal is to share:

- UI components
- Localization
- Search/filter logic
- API client
- Place profiles
- User-preference logic

Native Android functionality can later add improved GPS integration, camera access, sharing, notifications, and offline capabilities without rebuilding the product from scratch.

### 13.3 Map stack

Initial map rendering:

- MapLibre GL JS
- OpenStreetMap-derived map data
- OpenFreeMap as the initial hosted map source

The map-provider boundary must remain replaceable. A future self-hosted PMTiles/Protomaps setup should be possible if traffic, cost, or control requirements change.

### 13.4 Backend

Recommended initial backend:

- PostgreSQL
- PostGIS
- Supabase for managed hosting/auth/storage/API during the early phase

PostGIS handles geographic queries such as:

- Places within a radius
- Places inside the current viewport
- Nearest matching places

The application should avoid relying on Supabase-specific data structures where ordinary PostgreSQL/PostGIS equivalents suffice, preserving a future self-hosting path.

### 13.5 Search abstraction

External place/geocoding search must sit behind a `SearchService` abstraction.

Initial provider may use Nominatim conservatively, with caching and adherence to public-service usage limits. The abstraction allows replacement with another provider or a self-hosted service without redesigning the application.

Need-based search is handled by SENSEMAP itself by translating phrases into structured category/attribute filters.

## 14. Suggested core data model

The exact schema will be finalized during implementation planning, but the conceptual boundaries are:

### `places`

- Internal place ID
- OpenStreetMap identifier(s), where applicable
- Name
- Category
- Geographic position
- Address
- Source metadata

### `place_facts`

Persistent factual attributes such as toilets, step-free access, quiet room, and seating.

### `sense_reports`

Structured user observations covering noise, light, crowding, smell, accessibility, and related sensory attributes.

### `live_reports`

Short-lived current-condition observations with `created_at` and `expires_at`.

### `comments`

Optional account-authenticated short text notes subject to moderation/reporting.

### `users`

Minimal contribution account data. Avoid unnecessary demographic or sensory-profile data.

### `moderation_items`

Queue entries for reported content, new places, and significant disputed changes.

## 15. Privacy architecture

Principles:

- Precise GPS location is processed only as needed for nearby search and is not retained by default.
- Sensory preferences are local-device data by default.
- Do not build behavioural location histories.
- Do not create public contributor activity trails.
- Collect the minimum account information necessary for contribution/moderation.
- Analytics, if added, must be privacy-conscious and non-essential to basic operation.

## 16. SENSEMAP v0.1 scope

### Included

- OpenStreetMap-based map and place discovery
- Place search
- Need-based search
- Sensory filters
- Place profile / “Know before you go”
- Current-condition reports
- Structured contributions from logged-in users
- Nynorsk and English
- Local personal-needs profile
- Personal-fit ranking
- Report spam/incorrect information
- Basic moderation queue
- Mobile-friendly PWA

### Explicitly deferred

- Play Store Android release
- Image uploads
- Verified businesses/organizations
- Advanced reputation/gamification
- Push notifications
- Full offline maps
- Social feeds/followers/likes
- Public contributor profiles
- AI chatbot
- External real-time crowd feeds
- Languages beyond Nynorsk and English

These features must not delay the first useful release.

## 17. v0.1 success criteria

### Visitor flow

A first-time visitor should be able to:

1. Open SENSEMAP.
2. Allow location access or search manually.
3. See nearby places.
4. Select needs such as low noise + low crowding + seating.
5. Receive relevant place matches.
6. Open a place and understand the important environmental characteristics within approximately 10 seconds.

### Contributor flow

A logged-in contributor should be able to go from:

> “I am here now”

to a submitted structured report in under one minute.

### Product-quality criteria

- No basic browsing feature requires login.
- A single report cannot define an established place profile.
- Current reports expire automatically.
- Nynorsk and English have complete UI coverage.
- Personal needs do not require server-side storage.
- The application remains usable on a typical mobile browser.

## 18. Guiding non-goals

SENSEMAP v0.1 is **not**:

- A universal safety-rating service
- A social network
- A review site competing on star ratings
- A medical diagnostic tool
- A venue-certification authority
- A commercial advertising platform

It is a practical map that helps people answer one question:

> **What is this place likely to be like for me?**
